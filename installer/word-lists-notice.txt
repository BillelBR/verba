Verba - word lists and their license

Verba suggests words from frequency lists that are installed in the "data\words" folder next to the program:

    en.txt   English
    fr.txt   French
    ar.txt   Arabic (Modern Standard Arabic and everyday speech)

These lists come from the project "FrequencyWords" by hermitdave
(https://github.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords, 2018 lists), which builds them from the
OpenSubtitles corpus (https://opus.nlpl.eu/OpenSubtitles2018.php).

    License of the lists: Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 (CC-BY-SA-4.0)
    https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/

The lists are separate files, and they keep that license. Verba's own program is not covered by it.
You may replace them with your own lists in the same "word count" format.

The next-word tables (en.bigrams.txt, fr.bigrams.txt, ar.bigrams.txt) count which word follows which. They were built
from sentences of Tatoeba (https://tatoeba.org), (c) Tatoeba contributors, license Creative Commons Attribution 2.0 France
(CC BY 2.0 FR, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/fr/). The tables hold counts of word pairs only, no sentence.

The word-translation dictionary (dict.txt) lists translations between English, French and Arabic. It was built from Wiktionary
(https://en.wiktionary.org), (c) Wiktionary contributors, as extracted by wiktextract (https://kaikki.org, Tatu Ylonen).

    License of dict.txt: Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0), and GNU Free Documentation License (GFDL)
    https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/

Changed: the entries were reduced to short word translations, ranked, and inflected forms were added. dict.txt is a separate file and keeps
that license (you must keep the attribution and give a copy the same license); Verba's own program is not covered by it. Wiktionary's own
statement of its license: https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Wiktionary:Copyrights

Sentence translation (the optional "Sentence translation" part of this setup) uses a program of its own, verba-translate.exe, and two
translation models in the "models" folder (ar-en and en-ar). The models are OPUS-MT networks by the Language Technology Research Group
at the University of Helsinki (https://huggingface.co/Helsinki-NLP), converted to int8 for CTranslate2 (changed from the originals):

    en-ar  Helsinki-NLP/opus-mt-en-ar        License: Apache 2.0 (https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0)
    ar-en  Helsinki-NLP/opus-mt-tc-big-ar-en License: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0,
           https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), (c) the OPUS-MT authors, University of Helsinki;
           the corrected checkpoint of pull request 7 on Hugging Face was converted to int8.

The program contains CTranslate2 (MIT, SYSTRAN and the OpenNMT authors), oneDNN (Apache 2.0, Intel) and SentencePiece (Apache 2.0, Google);
vcomp140.dll is a redistributable file of Microsoft. Details and the other libraries: THIRD-PARTY.md. A translation program makes mistakes:
check a translation before you rely on it. Algerian Darija is not translated by it.

Privacy: Verba reads only the few letters before the caret in the text box you are typing in, never in password
boxes, keeps nothing, and sends nothing over the network.

More details: DATA.md in the installation folder.
